Sales Team Mobile App & Management Dashboard
A Simple Guide to What the App Will Do
Prepared for easy reading — no technical terms, just what you and your team will see and use.
 
What Is This App, in One Minute?
Right now, when a sales officer goes out to visit schools and shops, Bookmark has no real way of knowing where they went, how long they stayed, or whether they actually did the visit at all. This app fixes that problem completely.
There will be two connected parts:
•	1. Sales Officer App — A mobile app for the sales officers themselves, used every day in the field.
•	2. Management Dashboard — A web dashboard for Bookmark management, used to plan, watch, approve and review everything the field team does.
Below is a simple, feature-by-feature explanation of both, written the way you would see them on screen — not the technical version.
PART 1 — The Sales Officer's Mobile App
This is the app that every field sales officer will have on their phone. It guides their entire working day from start to finish.
1. Logging In
There is no “sign up” button anywhere in the app. An officer cannot create their own account. Bookmark's office creates every account and hands the officer their login details. If someone forgets their password, there is a “forgot password” option to recover it, and the officer can change their password any time from inside the app.
2. Starting the Day
Every morning, the officer must tap “Start Day” before they can do anything else. This is a hard rule — no visit, no activity, nothing can happen until the day is officially started. This is what lets Bookmark know for certain who showed up to work and when.
Note: If it's raining heavily or something else stops an officer from working, they can mark the day as “could not work” with a reason, instead of just staying silent. If an officer simply does not start the day with no explanation, it is automatically counted as a day of leave.
3. The Day's Visit List — Auto-Planned Every Night
The officer never has to figure out on their own which schools or shops to visit. Every night at midnight, the system automatically builds tomorrow's list of exactly 7 visits for each officer, in the smartest possible order (so they aren't zig-zagging across the city). The list is built like this: (this activity must not be performed so late so it could be done around 8:00pm etc)
•	Priority pick — If Bookmark's office has assigned a specific, important visit to this officer, that goes to the top of the list.
•	High-value schools — Two top-priority schools from their area.
•	Regular schools — Two medium-priority schools from their area.
•	Book shops — Two book shops from their area.
If a visit was missed the day before, or a customer had asked the officer to “come back next Wednesday,” the system remembers this automatically and slots it into the right day — the officer does not need to remember anything themselves. (this reminder must pop-up a day before the actual visit date)
4. Doing a Visit — Live, On the Map
When the officer taps to start a visit, three things happen automatically:
•	Navigation — The app opens turn-by-turn map directions straight to the school or shop.
•	Time tracking — The system starts recording how long it takes to get there and how long the officer stays.
•	Live tracking for the office — Bookmark's office can watch the officer's live location on a map in real time — exactly like watching a driver's location in a ride-hailing app.
This timing and location information is locked once recorded — the officer cannot edit or fake it afterwards.
5. Recording What Happened at the Visit
After each visit, the officer fills in a simple form: who they met, that person's job title and phone number, notes on the conversation, whether any free samples were given out, and what type of visit it was. If the officer is in a rush between calls, they can finish this later the same day — the app lets them close a visit quickly and come back to fill in the details afterwards, any time before the day is done. (We should give them a limited access to the dashboard for filling some data & visit report etc) 
6. Extra, Unplanned Visits
If an officer passes by a school that wasn't on today's list and decides to pop in, they can start a brand-new visit on the spot. It gets tracked exactly the same way as a planned visit, and is simply labelled as an “extra” visit in the reports. (We should also record this new data with his name as his extra effort & we can give him some reward for this new discovery, but this will only happen if the data is not previously available in our database)
7. Missed Visits — With Proof, Not Excuses
Sometimes a visit genuinely cannot happen — flooding, a road blockage, a closed school. In that case the officer must take a photo of the situation and type a short reason. This is sent straight to their City Head (their supervisor) to review.
•	Reason accepted — If the City Head accepts the reason, there is no penalty at all.
•	Reason rejected — If the City Head does not accept it, a penalty is applied and the officer can see it directly on their own profile.
A missed visit is never simply dropped — it stays on the list and shows how many times it has already been attempted (for example, “1st attempt”), so nothing quietly falls through the cracks.
8. Requesting Free Samples
Every officer has a yearly “budget” for free samples (for example, worth Rs. 300,000 for the year). When they want to give samples to a school, they request it in the app, the value is worked out automatically, and it goes for approval before it can be handed over. Once approved, it's subtracted from their yearly budget automatically — so nobody can hand out unlimited free samples.
After a sample is given, the app reminds the officer (after 10 days, then again after 20 days) to confirm whether it was returned or converted into a sale. If it's never confirmed, its value is quietly deducted from the officer's next salary — closing the loophole where samples simply disappear with no accountability. (We haven’t discussed the inventory module, the inventory means the products. How would you enter products?)
9. Applying for Leave
Every officer gets 28 days of leave a year (10 sick + 18 casual), and they can apply for it directly from the app rather than calling the office. Their remaining balance is always visible to them.
10. Seeing Their Own Performance and Pay
Officers can see their own calendar of completed visits, any penalties they've received, and how their salary is being calculated — so there are no surprises at payday. (We must mention the Salary break-up i.e. Basic Salary, Security Deposit & Running Salary etc) 
11. A Little Daily Encouragement
To keep morale up, the app sends small motivational messages through the day — a “good luck” message at the start, a “halfway there” nudge, a “well done” at the end, plus a short sales tip or quote of the day. These are kept brief so they never get in the way of actual work.
 
PART 2 — The Management Dashboard (Web Panel)
This is the screen Bookmark's office team, City Heads and Administrators will use on a computer to run the entire sales operation.
1. Setting Up the Team
The Administrator adds every new sales officer into the system — their personal details, which city and area they cover, who their City Head is, their salary structure, and their sample budget for the year. Login details are generated and handed to the officer at this point.
2. Assigning Priority Visits
If a customer calls the office asking for someone to visit, the office team (Coordinator) can create that visit request right away and send it directly to the correct area officer, along with instructions on what to bring and what to discuss.
3. Watching the Team Live
At any moment, management can open the map and see exactly which officers are currently on a visit and where they are — live. This alone solves Bookmark's biggest problem today: not knowing what the field team is actually doing.
4. Reviewing and Approving Missed Visits
Whenever an officer submits a photo and reason for a missed visit, it lands in front of their City Head for approval or rejection. The Administrator can see and, if needed, overrule any of these decisions across the whole company.
5. Controlling Samples
Management approves every sample request before it goes out, and can see a full running ledger — which officer gave which sample, to which school, worth how much, and whether it ever came back. This turns an uncontrolled cost into something fully tracked.
6. Reports and Dashboards
Management can filter everything by city, by area, or by date range, and can click into any single visit to see its full story — timing, route taken, who was met, and what was discussed. They can also look up any single school or shop and see its entire visit history across every officer who has ever called on it, over time. (We also need some graphical presentations too)
7. Salary, Deductions and Security Deposit
The dashboard automatically works out each officer's pay based on three parts: a fixed basic salary, a security deposit that builds up and is paid out at year-end, and a daily performance amount that is only earned on days the officer completes their visits (or has an approved reason if they didn't). All of this is visible and auditable by the Administrator.
 
Why This Matters for Bookmark
Every feature above exists to answer one of the real problems Bookmark faces today:
•	“I don't know what my sales team is doing all day” — Live tracking and locked timings mean management always knows where the team is and what they did.
•	“Some schools get visited too much, others never” — The auto-generated nightly visit plan makes sure important schools are never skipped and less important ones aren't over-visited.
•	“I can't tell excuses from real problems” — Photo + written proof, reviewed by a City Head, replaces guesswork with evidence.
•	“Samples go out and we never see the value again” — A hard yearly limit, approval step, and automatic follow-up closes the gap completely.
•	“People get paid whether they work or not” — Pay is now tied directly to whether the day's work was actually done.
•	“We can't supervise cities where we have no office” — Because everything runs through the app and the cloud, a City Head does not need to physically sit in every city for the system to work.
